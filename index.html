Mass trading

Trade offer manager

Market buying

Place buy orders straight from the dashboard. Search any item, pull its live price, and SSIM confirms the order. It rests until it fills.

Market selling

List at the lowest price, an undercut, or a fixed net payout. You see exactly what lands in your wallet before anything goes live.

Open orders and mass buy

Track every open buy and sell order across all accounts and cancel any of them in place. Queue a mass buy that checks balances before it runs.

Value history

Every refresh records your item worth and wallet balance, so the curve tracks your real holdings per environment and across everything.

Storage units

Casket contents are read and counted, not left as one opaque item. Move things in and out, and SSIM checks the move actually happened.

Per-account proxies

Encrypted vault

Security and trust

Your credentials stay on your PC.

You are handing this app your Steam accounts, so here is plainly how they are looked after.

Runs on your machine

Your passwords and 2FA secrets go to Steam when you sign in, and nowhere else. No server of ours is involved.

Locked with your password

You set a master password on first launch, and everything sensitive on disk is encrypted with it. Someone copying the folder gets nothing usable.

Nothing is uploaded

No sign-up, no tracking, no usage reports. SSIM talks to Steam, to CSFloat if you turn it on, and to GitHub to check for updates.

The dashboard is private

It runs on your own computer and is not reachable from the internet. Putting it on your network is something you have to deliberately turn on.

Trades confirmed on your device

The 2FA step that approves a trade or a listing happens locally. Your authenticator secrets are only ever used on your own PC.

Each account kept apart

Every account gets its own connection, and its own proxy if you set one. Sessions do not mix, so one account cannot affect another.

Anyone can read the code

SSIM is open source. You do not have to take any of this on trust, and you do not have to be a programmer to have someone check it for you.

Downloads you can check

Every release is built automatically from the public source and published with a fingerprint you can compare. How to check it.

FAQ

Common questions

Is it really free?+

Yes, and it stays free. SSIM has never been sold, and it is now open source under the Apache License 2.0. There is no paid tier, no trial, and nothing held back.

Do I need an account, a licence key or an invite?+

None of them. During the closed beta a licence server limited access to invited testers. The beta is over and that gate is gone. Download it and open it.

I was in the closed beta. What changes for me?+

Update to the current version and everything carries on. The licence check is gone, any licence files you still have are simply ignored, and your accounts, vault and history all stay where they are. Updates now come from GitHub. See coming from a closed-beta build.

What do I need to get started?+

Windows 10 or 11, about 500 MB of space, and the Steam accounts you control. You can sign in with a QR code from the Steam mobile app. If you add an account's .maFile, SSIM can also confirm trades and listings for you instead of you approving each one on your phone.

Is it safe to give it my 2FA secrets?+

That is the right question to ask of any tool like this. Everything stays on your own PC, encrypted with a master password only you know, and none of it is ever uploaded. Because SSIM is open source, you or anyone you trust can check that this is true rather than believing a website.

Where is my data stored?+

In folders next to the app, on your own machine. Your accounts and their secrets sit in an encrypted vault protected by your master password. There is no cloud account and nothing to sync.

How do updates work?+

SSIM checks for a newer release when it starts and can install it before opening. It checks the file is genuine first and refuses anything that does not match, so an update cannot be swapped for something else.

Does Steam allow this?+

Steam's Subscriber Agreement restricts automated access, and SSIM automates actions on Steam accounts. It uses the same public web endpoints your browser does. Use it on accounts you own, and make your own call on whether that suits you.

Does it support both CS2 and TF2?+

Yes. CS2 has the full set: inventory, trading and the Market. TF2 inventories are supported as a read-only view.

Can I use proxies?+

Yes, HTTP or SOCKS, set per account or for a whole environment. Each account uses its own connection, and a built-in checker shows the exit IP, country and speed of each one. See the proxies guide.
