# Code of Conduct

## The short version

Be decent to people. This is a small project; we can keep this simple.

## What's expected

- **Assume good faith.** Most friction is a misunderstanding, a language barrier,
  or someone having a bad day. Not everyone here writes English as a first
  language, and that's fine.
- **Critique the work, not the person.** "This has a race condition on the second
  call" is useful. "Did you even test this?" is not.
- **Remember people are volunteering.** Nobody is paid for this. A maintainer who
  hasn't replied in two weeks isn't ignoring you; a contributor whose PR needs
  three rounds of review isn't incompetent.
- **Beginners are welcome.** Everyone was one. "Obvious" questions are how people
  learn, and an unanswered one usually means our docs are bad.

## What isn't acceptable

- Harassment, insults, or personal attacks
- Discrimination or demeaning comments about who someone is: nationality, race,
  gender, sexuality, religion, disability, or age
- Sexual language or imagery
- Publishing someone's private information without their permission
- Sustained disruption, brigading, or bad-faith argument
- **Using this project's spaces to distribute malware, scam builds, phishing links,
  or stolen credentials.** Given what SSIM handles, this gets an immediate,
  permanent ban with no warning.

## Scope

Applies in every project space: the repository, issues, pull requests, discussions,
and the project Discord. It also applies when you're publicly representing the
project elsewhere.

## Reporting

Report problems to the maintainer on Discord (`Santer.xyz`), or through GitHub's
own [report abuse](https://github.com/contact/report-abuse) flow if the problem is
the maintainer.

Reports are handled privately. We won't share your identity with the person you're
reporting without asking you first.

## Enforcement

Maintainers may remove comments, close issues or PRs, and ban accounts as needed.
In rough order: a private word, a public warning, a temporary ban, a permanent one.
Serious cases skip straight to the end. The malware clause above especially.

We aim to be proportionate, and we'd rather resolve something with a quiet message
than a ban. But this is a small project without the capacity for prolonged
mediation, so persistent problems get resolved bluntly in favour of everyone else.
